## The target

- **Primary query:** `why copywriting templates don't work`
- **The AEO question a real person types:** "I bought so many templates and swipe files — why does my copy still sound generic?"
- **Who's searching:** Chloe at 11pm, surrounded by purchased templates that didn't fix it. She is not anti-template — she's embarrassed it didn't work and suspects it's her fault.

## The angle that wins (build to this, not the generic topic)

Every strong incumbent argues the copywriter-gatekeeping side ("templates threaten professionals"). Nobody answers the **buyer's** side. We take her side: the template was never the problem, and neither is she — the sequence was. A template is a container. It arranges words; it cannot supply hers. Voice and message clarity must exist BEFORE any template can work. That is the whole piece.

**Draft direct answer (open the script AND the post with this, 40–60 words):**

> Copywriting templates don't work on their own because a template is a container, not a message. It arranges words — it can't supply yours. If you're not clear on your voice and message first, every template fills up with the same borrowed phrases everyone else uses. Get the message clear first, and templates finally start working.

## Structure — question-shaped H2s, each opening with a mini-answer

1. **Why do copywriting templates leave your copy sounding generic?**
   The container/content mechanism. The template did its job — it held words. The words it held weren't hers, because hers weren't clear yet.

2. **Is it the template's fault — or yours? (Neither.)**
   Kill the shame directly. She followed the instructions; the instructions skipped a step. The industry sells step 2 (containers) without step 1 (her message). Not a character flaw, a sequence error.

3. **How can you tell whether the problem is the template or the message?**
   *Extractable structure #1 — a 5-check self-diagnostic (checklist format):* e.g., Can you say who you help, the problem, and the result in one breath — without the template? · Do your filled-in templates sound like your voice notes to a friend? · Could a longtime client pick YOUR filled template out of three? · Did you rewrite the template's stock phrases or leave them? · When your offer changed, did the copy change with it? Each check states what a NO means.

4. **What should you do before buying one more template?**
   *Extractable structure #2 — a 3-step recovery sequence (numbered):* pull your real words from where they already exist (voice notes, client conversations, testimonials) → name the one-sentence message → THEN bring templates back as speed, not source. Framed as recovery/extraction of a voice she already has — never "construct a voice" with adjective charts.

5. **When do templates actually work?** *(honest-limits section)*
   After message clarity exists, templates are genuine accelerators. We are not anti-template and not anti-education — we're anti-sequence-error. This honesty is the trust move the incumbents skip.

**FAQ candidates (pick 3–5 at draft time, in-territory only):** Should I throw my templates away? · Are expensive templates better than free ones? · Why does every website in my niche sound the same? · How long does it take to get message-clear before templates help?
