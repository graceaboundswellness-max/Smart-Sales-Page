# Blog Post Template — SEO / AEO / AI Overviews / GEO

The structure every blog post follows. Optimizes four surfaces at once: classic ranking (SEO), answer engines and snippets (AEO), and being quoted by AI Overviews / ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity (AIO/GEO).

Target length **1,800–3,000 words** — a range, not a quota. Length follows substance; the cut pass (§14) runs before the checklist. Reading level **grade 7**. Register: truth over hype.

---

## PHASE 0 — Before writing a word

**0a. Name the asset's job.** Ranking asset (targets a keyword; answer-first, tables, FAQ) · Authority asset (builds entity/E-E-A-T; story-led, heavy Person schema) · Conversion asset (sells; persuasion first). A story-led piece is an authority asset — don't dress it as a ranking asset and expect it to rank.

**0b. Pull the keyword from demand, never from the content.** Source: `research/content-topics.md`, the Chloe Keyword Bank, or her VOC search language. Never write the piece first and pick a keyword to fit it.

**0c. Search the keyword before committing.** Who owns page one (unwinnable domains?) · Does searcher intent match what this post will be? · What's the gap nobody serves? Territory answers are in `research/open-lanes.md`; that field data is July 2026, so re-check the live SERP before investing.

**0d. CTA-intent match.** The reader landing on this keyword must be one plausible step from the offer. If not, the keyword is wrong even if winnable.

## 1 · Metadata block (fill completely — zero placeholders)

```
Asset job:            [ranking / authority / conversion]
Territory:            [from research/content-topics.md]
Primary query:        [exact phrase, from demand]
AEO question:         [the human-phrased version she actually types]
The gap I'm filling:  [1 sentence — from open-lanes.md or your own SERP check]
Post title (H1):      [see title patterns below]
SEO title:            [50–60 characters — Google truncates ~60]
Meta description:     [150–160 chars, contains the direct answer, not a teaser]
URL slug:             [lowercase-hyphens, exact-match-ish, short]
Category:             [one of the three pillars: unbury-your-message / ai-without-the-ick / safe-to-be-seen]
Author:               Wanda Lambert [+ one-line credential]
Published / Updated:  [real dates]
```

Title patterns that win these SERPs: question-form, number + fix-promise, or first-person proof ("What I Know After Rebuilding Five Times"). Faith qualifier: allowed when the territory supports it.

**Audience age is internal targeting only.** "55–65" never appears in copy or titles. If the target query itself contains an age ("starting an online business at 55"), flag it in Phase 0 and get Wanda's explicit yes before the title uses it.

Categories are the three content pillars — never invent a second taxonomy. Truth Over Hype is a thread through everything, not a category. Brand name on the category label; search language in the category description (that's where the SEO weight sits).

## 2 · The Direct Answer (first 2–3 sentences — non-negotiable)

A complete 40–60 word answer to the AEO question, before any story or setup. Must work if lifted out entirely — this is what snippets, AI Overviews, and chatbots quote. Plain declarative verdict, never "it depends."

## 3 · Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets, directly under the Direct Answer)

Each bullet is a distinct, standalone claim from the post — not a teaser, not one point restated five ways. Specific numbers where they exist. If a bullet can't survive being quoted alone, out of context, rewrite it until it can. Bullet lists are a second extraction surface; AI answers lift them more readily than prose.

Set this block's heading as an **H3**, not an H2, so the every-H2-is-a-question rule stays clean.

## 4 · The Mirror (2–4 short paragraphs)

Her exact situation in her symptom words — she searches "why isn't anyone buying," not "brand messaging help." Symptom language leads cold-reader sections because it mirrors her search behavior. "Messaging" is not banned; use it where it reads naturally. No throat-clearing.

## 5 · Why the usual answers fail (1 question-shaped H2)

Name what she's already tried or read and say precisely why it didn't work. Contradict the incumbents specifically — `research/open-lanes.md` maps what they say — never vaguely.

## 6 · The Core Teaching (2–4 question-shaped H2s)

- Each H2 is a question a searcher would type, opening with a 1–2 sentence direct answer, then teaching in voice.
- **At least TWO extractable structures across the post**: tables, checklists, numbered processes, decision frameworks. These are what AI answers pull; prose alone rarely is. Where the topic has a natural comparison, make one of them a comparison table — comparison-shaped content shows the strongest measured association with being pulled into AI answers.
- Specific numbers over vague claims. Name the framework and use the name consistently; named frameworks get cited.
- Put finished words in the reader's mouth where possible — an actual one-liner, an actual rewritten sentence.
- **Claim-level reuse:** every non-obvious claim sits in a self-contained 2–4 sentence paragraph carrying its own measurement, scope, and source. AI engines quote chunks, not pages — a claim leaning on the previous paragraph doesn't travel.
- **Outbound citations, 3–5 across the post:** link to the specific article, study, or documentation page — never a homepage — inline at the claim it supports. Authoritative, non-competing sources only; never the incumbents this post contradicts.

## 7 · Story = evidence, not spine (ranking assets)

One section, **≤300 words**, positioned AFTER the practical content, doing E-E-A-T work. If the story is the spine, it's an authority asset — label it as one in Phase 0 and stop expecting it to rank.

## 8 · The Honest Limits (1 short paragraph)

What this won't fix, and when it doesn't apply. Truth Over Hype made structural — it pre-handles the skeptical reader before she has to raise the objection herself.

## 9 · FAQ (3–5 questions) — visible on the page

Phrase the FAQ heading itself as a question ("Still have questions about [topic]?"), since it's an H2 and the H2 rule applies. Use verbatim-phrased territory questions; each answer 2–4 sentences, standalone.

The **visible** FAQ does the AEO work — the schema was never the mechanism. FAQPage rich results were removed May 2026; markup is harmless but optional. AI Overviews require no special markup, only that structured data match visible text.

## 10 · CTA (one, quiet)

One CTA, quoted from the live offer sheet — never improvised or described from memory (see the offer rule in the workflow). Awareness territories → free asset or Substack · Diagnosis territories → diagnostic, then the DFY doorway · AI territories → the "Your voice first" cornerstone or DFY front door. One sentence of why, one link, no scarcity energy.

## 11 · Author box + freshness footer

Named author, 2–3 sentence bio with load-bearing credentials, real photo, visible "Last updated [date]."

## 12 · Images — plan + generation prompts (deliver with every post)

**Informational > decorative.** A formula graphic or comparison table gets pulled into image search and AI cards; a stock laptop photo just slows the page. Send anything with words to the image GPT first — it handles type well. Canva is the fallback only if type comes out wrong.

**The answer object.** For posts built on steps, frameworks, processes, diagnostics, comparisons, formulas, or checklists — most ranking assets — one in-post graphic is a single comprehensive infographic showing the COMPLETE idea in one visual. The production principle: *blog article = searchable text explanation + one visually complete answer object.* Two rules travel with it:

- **The text never depends on the pixels.** Every substantive step in the graphic must also exist as HTML text near it. The §6 extractable structures already do this job; the graphic packages them, never replaces them. Google reads alt text, surrounding copy, captions, and (less so) filenames — give it all of them.
- **The cover and the infographic have different jobs.** Google advises against text-heavy images as the social/OG preview, and the cover doubles as the OG image. Cover = clean visual, minimal words (a short title phrase at most, or none). Infographic = the text-dense answer object, inline only, never promoted to the preview slot.

The answer object is a production rule, not a ranking promise: the supporting research is associative, and no study shows an infographic causes better rankings.

For EACH image, the draft must deliver this block:

```
IMAGE [n] — [cover / in-post]
Concept prompt:  [the concept + any words that must appear — NOTHING about colors,
                  fonts, or style; the image GPT holds the brand and decides]
Context:         [paste the post section this image supports, so the generator
                  understands the idea it's illustrating]
Size:            [cover: 1200×630px (doubles as social share) · in-post: 1200px wide]
File name:       lowercase-hyphens-with-keyword.webp   (never IMG_4471.png; WebP ≈ ⅓ PNG size, and speed is a ranking factor)
Alt text:        [describes the image content, works the keyword in naturally, ≤125 chars]
Caption:         [in-post infographics only: one line under the graphic, keyword natural]
```

Cover prompts specify minimal or no words; in-post infographic prompts carry the complete framework text. Cover image is required; 1–2 in-post informational graphics max. In GHL, insert images through the editor so they land as standard crawlable `<img>` elements — never as background styling.

**YouTube embed (the source video):** embed near the top — after the Mirror or the first core-teaching H2 — with a 2–3 sentence summary of the video directly beneath it. Use an HTML block in GHL and leave the block label blank, or it renders as visible text. Then close the loop the other way: the live blog URL goes into the video's YouTube description.

## 13 · Schema — Article + Person, at the BLOG level (not the post body)

GHL sanitizes script tags in the post body, so schema goes at blog level. Skip HowTo entirely (rich results removed 2023). FAQPage is optional and harmless. What matters:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "Article",
      "headline": "[SEO title]",
      "author": { "@id": "#wanda" },
      "datePublished": "[real ISO date]",
      "dateModified": "[real ISO date]"
    },
    {
      "@type": "Person",
      "@id": "#wanda",
      "name": "Wanda Lambert",
      "url": "[founder page URL]",
      "knowsAbout": ["conversion copywriting", "brand voice", "AI writing", "..."],
      "sameAs": ["[Substack]", "[YouTube]", "[Facebook @wandaruizlambert]", "..."],
      "award": ["Distinguished Executive of the Year (December 1999)", "International Educator of the Year (×2)"]
    }
  ]
}
```

Fill knowable fields — `datePublished`, `sameAs`, socials are knowable, and omitting them weakens the entity work. Omit truly unknown ones. **Never ship placeholders.** Structured data must match visible text.

## 14 · Formatting discipline

Bold **4 things max** per post. No highlighter. Dividers 1–3 total, not between every H2. 2–4 internal links to pillar posts with descriptive anchors. Any affiliate or monetized link gets `rel="sponsored"`.

**The cut pass — run it before the checklist.** Delete section openers that restate their heading, "as I mentioned" callbacks, recaps that re-list every rule, and hedge padding. Nothing survives that exists only to reach word count.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Phase 0 done: asset job named, keyword from demand, SERP checked (owner/intent/gap), CTA one step away
- [ ] Direct answer in first 3 sentences, standalone and quotable, 40–60 words
- [ ] Key Takeaways: 3–5 bullets under the direct answer, each a distinct standalone claim, as an H3
- [ ] Every H2 is a typed question and opens with a mini-answer (the FAQ heading included)
- [ ] ≥2 extractable structures (table/checklist/numbered process/framework)
- [ ] Story ≤300 words, after the practical content (ranking assets)
- [ ] Symptom language leads the cold-reader sections
- [ ] No audience age in copy or titles without Wanda's explicit Phase 0 approval
- [ ] ≥3 specific numbers; zero vague quantifiers; zero hype-register phrases
- [ ] 3–5 outbound citations: specific pages, never homepages, inline at the claim, non-competing
- [ ] Cut pass done: no heading-restating openers, no callbacks, no recaps, no padding
- [ ] Bold ≤4, dividers ≤3, no highlighter
- [ ] SEO title 50–60 chars; meta description 150–160 with the answer in it
- [ ] Visible FAQ present; schema = Article + Person, blog-level, no placeholders, matches visible text
- [ ] Image block complete for every image: concept prompt (no colors/style) + context + size + filename + alt text (+ caption for in-post infographics)
- [ ] Answer object: framework/steps/comparison posts have ONE complete-concept inline infographic, and every lesson in it also exists as body text
- [ ] Cover is minimal-text (it doubles as the OG/social preview)
- [ ] YouTube embed near the top with a 2–3 sentence video summary (when a source video exists)
- [ ] CTA quotes the live offer sheet, with the quoted row shown in the receipts
- [ ] Claims check against `research/positioning.md`; facts check against the canonical-facts table in `publishing-playbook.md`
- [ ] Research Receipts block appended (3–7 lines: finding → decision)
- [ ] Publish body is clean: zero internal blocks in the paste-ready body — no OFFER SOURCE, receipts, checklist, metadata, or image prompts

For the GHL publish itself — field order, gotchas, verification — follow `publishing-playbook.md` Phases 2–5.
